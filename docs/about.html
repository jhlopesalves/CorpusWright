Where CorpusWright stands

CorpusWright is built on one position: the description of a corpus is not a preliminary step taken before statistical analysis; it is the first stage of statistical analysis. The data-science tradition has a name for this stage: exploratory data analysis (Tukey, 1977), the work of understanding a dataset's structure, its distribution, and its limits before any confirmatory claim is made. CorpusWright's response is to place descriptive diagnostics in the analysis workflow, where they can be examined before later claims are made.

The position is not novel, and the project does not claim it as its own. The project draws on ordinary open-science practices - reproducible results, transparent methods, findings that carry their provenance, and claims held to organised scepticism - in corpus preparation and analysis. In practice, that discipline means five things:

  • Deterministic. For the same supported inputs and cleaning configuration, the cleaning rules produce the same processed text. A saved cleaning configuration is part of the research record: it can be inspected, shared, and re-applied without relying on anyone's recollection of manual steps.
  • Conservative. Where a cleaning decision is uncertain, the original text is preserved. Detected noise is evidence, not an instruction; it becomes a cleaning rule only after explicit review. The tool would rather under-clean than silently discard what cannot be recovered.
  • Auditable. Extraction warnings, cleaning rules and export metadata remain inspectable, because changing tokenisation or annotation choices can change the counts.
  • Local-first. Corpus data is processed locally and is not sent to a hosted CorpusWright analysis service.
  • Statistically restrained. Frequencies are reported as what they are, without overstatement; measures respect corpus design, dispersion, document-level variation, and their own uncertainty.

Established methods in the workflow

The project does not claim novelty for established measures such as Zipf’s law (Zipf, 1949) or Shannon entropy (Shannon, 1948), nor for the principles of careful corpus design. Its purpose is to place corpus preparation and descriptive diagnostics in one local workflow and make the relevant settings available for review.

Maintainer

CorpusWright started as a solo project and is maintained, for now, by the person who started it.

The short version of the origin: as an undergraduate on an iniciação científica project, I was given eight to twelve months to clean a corpus one file at a time; a few weeks of Python reduced the job to days - and the corpus still went barely examined. CorpusWright is the long answer to that experience.
