About CorpusWright

An open-source corpus-linguistics workbench, created and maintained by Jhonatan H. Lopes.

What CorpusWright is

CorpusWright is desktop software for preparing text corpora, examining their statistical structure and exploring words in context. It brings text extraction, reviewed cleaning rules, descriptive measures and Concordance into one workflow. Corpus files are processed on your computer.

The Guide covers installation and the basic workflow. Methods & measures explains the definitions, diagnostic figures and limits on interpretation.

Why it exists

As an undergraduate on an iniciação científica project, I was given eight to twelve months to clean a corpus one file at a time. A few weeks of Python reduced the job to days, and the corpus still went barely examined. CorpusWright is the longer response to that experience.

Preparation and examination belong together: the text that survives cleaning determines what can be counted, and the counts need to be read against the documents that produced them.

Methodological stance

The project treats corpus description as part of statistical analysis. Descriptive diagnostics help examine a corpus's structure, distribution and limits before later claims are made. Frequency alone does not establish how a word is distributed, nor whether a corpus is appropriate for a research question.

The workflow keeps cleaning decisions inspectable, records settings and warnings, and includes preparation metadata with cleaned-corpus exports. Methods documents how the numbers are produced.

Citation

If you use CorpusWright in research or teaching, cite the software and the version you used. A human-readable template is:

Lopes, J. H. (release year). CorpusWright (Version version used) [Computer software]. https://github.com/jhlopesalves/CorpusWright

Replace the year and version with the details of your release. The repository's CITATION.cff is the authoritative machine-readable record. APA and BibTeX examples follow its current metadata; check them against the version you used.

Report the relevant preparation and analysis settings alongside the software citation.

Status and licence

CorpusWright is a public experimental alpha: usable, changing and still rough in places. Project formats and behaviour may change between releases. Keep original sources and backups of project data, and consult the release notes for the version you use.

The source is available under the MIT licence. Bundled dependencies and resources have their own terms, recorded in the third-party notices.
